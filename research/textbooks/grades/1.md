**1. Sums and Differences to 10**

- _A. Embedded Numbers and Decompositions_
  - 1. Analyze and describe embedded numbers (to 10) using 5-groups and number bonds.
  - 2. Reason about embedded numbers in varied configurations using number bonds.
  - 3. See and describe numbers of objects using 1 more within 5-group configurations.
- _B. Counting On from Embedded Numbers_
  - 4–5. Represent put together situations with number bonds. Count on from one embedded number or part to totals of 6 and 7, and generate all addition expressions for each total.
  - 6–7. Represent put together situations with number bonds. Count on from one embedded number or part to totals of 8 and 9, and generate all expressions for each total.
  - 8. Represent all the number pairs of 10 as number bonds from a given scenario, and generate all expressions equal to 10.
- _C. Addition Word Problems_
  - 9. Solve add to with result unknown and put together with result unknown math stories by drawing, writing equations, and making statements of the solution.
  - 10. Solve put together with result unknown math stories by drawing and using 5-group cards.
  - 11. Solve add to with change unknown math stories as a context for counting on by drawing, writing equations, and making statements of the solution.
  - 12. Solve add to with change unknown math stories using 5-group cards.
  - 13. Tell put together with result unknown, add to with result unknown, and add to with change unknown stories from equations.
- _D. Strategies for Counting On_
  - 14–15. Count on up to 3 more using numeral and 5-group cards and fingers to track the change.
  - 16. Count on to find the unknown part in missing addend equations such as 6 + __ = 9. Answer, “How many more to make 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10?”
- _E. The Commutative Property of Addition and the Equal Sign_
  - 17–18. Understand the meaning of the equal sign by pairing equivalent expressions and constructing true number sentences.
  - 19. Represent the same story scenario with addends repositioned (the commutative property).
  - 20. Apply the commutative property to count on from a larger addend.
- _F. Development of Addition Fluency Within 10_
  - 21. Visualize and solve doubles and doubles plus 1 with 5-group cards.
  - 22. Look for and make use of repeated reasoning on the addition chart by solving and analyzing problems with common addends.
  - 23. Look for and make use of structure on the addition chart by looking for and coloring problems with the same total.
  - 24. Practice to build fluency with facts to 10.
- _G. Subtraction as an Unknown Addend Problem_
  - 25. Solve add to with change unknown math stories with addition, and relate to subtraction. Model with materials, and write corresponding number sentences.
  - 26–27. Count on using the number path to find an unknown part.
- _H. Subtraction Word Problems_
  - 28. Solve take from with result unknown math stories with math drawings, true number sentences, and statements, using horizontal marks to cross off what is taken away.
  - 29. Solve take apart with addend unknown math stories with math drawings, equations, and statements, circling the known part to find the unknown.
  - 30. Solve add to with change unknown math stories with drawings, relating addition and subtraction.
  - 31. Solve take from with change unknown math stories with drawings.
  - 32. Solve put together/take apart with addend unknown math stories.
- _I. Decomposition Strategies for Subtraction_
  - 33. Model 0 less and 1 less pictorially and as subtraction number sentences.
  - 34. Model n – n and n – (n – 1) pictorially and as subtraction sentences.
  - 35. Relate subtraction facts involving fives and doubles to corresponding decompositions.
  - 36. Relate subtraction from 10 to corresponding decompositions.
  - 37. Relate subtraction from 9 to corresponding decompositions.
- _J. Development of Subtraction Fluency Within 10_
  - 38. Look for and make use of repeated reasoning and structure, using the addition chart to solve subtraction problems.
  - 39. Analyze the addition chart to create sets of related addition and subtraction facts.

**2. Introduction to Place Value Through Addition and Subtraction Within 20**

- _A. Counting On or Making Ten to Solve Result Unknown and Total Unknown Problems_
  - 1. Solve word problems with three addends, two of which make ten.
  - 2. Use the associative and commutative properties to make ten with three addends.
  - 3–4. Make ten when one addend is 9.
  - 5. Compare efficiency of counting on and making ten when one addend is 9.
  - 6. Use the commutative property to make ten.
  - 7–8. Make ten when one addend is 8.
  - 9. Compare efficiency of counting on and making ten when one addend is 8.
  - 10. Solve problems with addends of 7, 8, and 9.
  - 11. Share and critique peer solution strategies for put together with total unknown word problems.
- _B. Counting On or Taking from Ten to Solve Result Unknown and Total Unknown Problems_
  - 12–13. Solve word problems with subtraction of 9 from 10.
  - 14–15. Model subtraction of 9 from teen numbers.
  - 16. Relate counting on to making ten and taking from ten.
  - 17–18. Model subtraction of 8 from teen numbers.
  - 19. Compare efficiency of counting on and taking from ten.
  - 20. Subtract 7, 8, and 9 from teen numbers.
  - 21. Share and critique peer solution strategies for take from with result unknown and take apart with addend unknown word problems from the teens.
- _C. Strategies for Solving Change or Addend Unknown Problems_
  - 22. Solve put together/take apart with addend unknown word problems, and relate counting on to the take from ten strategy.
  - 23. Solve add to with change unknown problems, relating varied addition and subtraction strategies.
  - 24. Strategize to solve take from with change unknown problems.
  - 25. Strategize and apply understanding of the equal sign to solve equivalent expressions.
- _D. Varied Problems with Decompositions of Teen Numbers as 1 Ten and Some Ones_
  - 26. Identify 1 ten as a unit by renaming representations of 10.
  - 27. Solve addition and subtraction problems decomposing and composing teen numbers as 1 ten and some ones.
  - 28. Solve addition problems using ten as a unit, and write two-step solutions.
  - 29. Solve subtraction problems using ten as a unit, and write two-step solutions.

**3. Ordering and Comparing Length Measurements as Numbers**

- _A. Indirect Comparison in Length Measurement_
  - 1. Compare length directly and consider the importance of aligning endpoints.
  - 2. Compare length using indirect comparison by finding objects longer than, shorter than, and equal in length to that of a string.
  - 3. Order three lengths using indirect comparison.
- _B. Standard Length Units_
  - 4. Express the length of an object using centimeter cubes as length units to measure with no gaps or overlaps.
  - 5. Rename and measure with centimeter cubes, using their standard unit name of centimeters.
  - 6. Order, measure, and compare the length of objects before and after measuring with centimeter cubes, solving compare with difference unknown word problems.
- _C. Non-Standard and Standard Length Units_
  - 7. Measure the same objects from Topic B with different non-standard units simultaneously to see the need to measure with a consistent unit.
  - 8. Understand the need to use the same units when comparing measurements with others.
  - 9. Answer compare with difference unknown problems about lengths of two different objects measured in centimeters.
- _D. Data Interpretation_
  - 10–11. Collect, sort, and organize data; then ask and answer questions about the number of data points.
  - 12–13. Ask and answer varied word problem types about a data set with three categories.

**4. Place Value, Comparison, Addition and Subtraction to 40**

- _A. Tens and Ones_
  - 1. Compare the efficiency of counting by ones and counting by tens.
  - 2. Use the place value chart to record and name tens and ones within a two-digit number.
  - 3. Interpret two-digit numbers as either tens and some ones or as all ones.
  - 4. Write and interpret two-digit numbers as addition sentences that combine tens and ones.
  - 5. Identify 10 more, 10 less, 1 more, and 1 less than a two-digit number.
  - 6. Use dimes and pennies as representations of tens and ones.
- _B. Comparison of Pairs of Two-Digit Numbers_

  - 8. Compare quantities and numerals from left to right.
  - 9–10. Use the symbols >, =, and < to compare quantities and numerals.
- _C. Addition and Subtraction of Tens_
  - 11. Add and subtract tens from a multiple of 10.
  - 12. Add tens to a two-digit number.
- _D. Addition of Tens or Ones to a Two-Digit Number_
  - 13–14. Use counting on and the make ten strategy when adding across a ten.
  - 15. Use single-digit sums to support solutions for analogous sums to 40.
  - 16–17. Add ones and ones or tens and tens.
  - 18. Share and critique peer strategies for adding two-digit numbers.
- _E. Varied Problem Types Within 20_
  - 19. Use tape diagrams as representations to solve put together/take apart with total unknown and add to with result unknown word problems.
  - 20–21. Recognize and make use of part–whole relationships within tape diagrams when solving a variety of problem types.
  - 22. Write word problems of varied types.
- _F. Addition of Tens and Ones to a Two-Digit Number_
  - 23. Interpret two-digit numbers as tens and ones, including cases with more than 9 ones.
  - 24–25. Add a pair of two-digit numbers when the ones digits have a sum less than or equal to 10.
  - 26–27. Add a pair of two-digit numbers when the ones digits have a sum greater than 10.
  - 28–29. Add a pair of two-digit numbers with varied sums in the ones.

**5. Identifying, Composing, and Partitioning Shapes**

- _A. Attributes of Shapes_
  - 1. Classify shapes based on defining attributes using examples, variants, and non-examples.
  - 2. Find and name two-dimensional shapes including trapezoid, rhombus, and a square as a special rectangle, based on defining attributes of sides and corners.
  - 3. Find and name three-dimensional shapes including cone and rectangular prism, based on defining attributes of faces and points.
- _B. Part–Whole Relationships Within Composite Shapes_
  - 4. Create composite shapes from two-dimensional shapes.
  - 5. Compose a new shape from composite shapes.
  - 6. Create a composite shape from three-dimensional shapes and describe the composite shape using shape names and positions.
- _C. Halves and Quarters of Rectangles and Circles_
  - 7. Name and count shapes as parts of a whole, recognizing relative sizes of the parts.
  - 8–9. Partition shapes and identify halves and quarters of circles and rectangles.
- _D. Application of Halves to Tell Time_
  - 10. Construct a paper clock by partitioning a circle and tell time to the hour.
  - 11–13. Recognize halves within a circular clock face and tell time to the half-hour.

**6. Place Value, Comparison, Addition and Subtraction to 100**

  - 1. Solve compare with difference unknown problem types.
  - 2. Solve compare with bigger or smaller unknown problem types.
- _B. Numbers to 120_
  - 3. Use the place value chart to record and name tens and ones within a two-digit number up to 100.
  - 4. Write and interpret two-digit numbers to 100 as addition sentences that combine tens and ones.
  - 5. Identify 10 more, 10 less, 1 more, and 1 less than a two-digit number within 100.
  - 6. Use the symbols >, =, and < to compare quantities and numerals to 100.
  - 7. Count and write numbers to 120. Use Hide Zero cards to relate numbers 0 to 20 to 100 to 120.

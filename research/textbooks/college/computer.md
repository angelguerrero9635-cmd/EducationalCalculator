# Computer: topics against open textbooks and courses

Reference only (see `../../README.md`): nothing here goes into a lesson, picture or test. Generated by the R4 crosswalk script from `../toc/college/*.json` and `../../questions/college/*.jsonl`; for each topic, the unit of every source that teaches it, in the source’s own order, with the worked-example kinds, number ranges and notation recorded in the table of contents (our words).

**What the sources show (2026-10-02).** Open computer sources reached: Levin's _Discrete Mathematics: An Open Introduction_, 3rd edition (CC BY-SA 4.0; the 4th edition is now CC BY-NC-SA), Morin's _Open Data Structures_ (CC BY 2.5 Canada), ModEL digital modules (CC BY 4.0), and MIT OpenCourseWare 6.004 and 6.006 (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). **Order and coverage.** Levin 3e runs statements, sets and functions → counting (including stars and bars with inclusion–exclusion) → sequences and recurrences → logic and proofs → graphs; its exercises are short, often interactive, and many are proofs. Morin's exercises are mostly implement-or-prove items with few numbers; heaps use the Eytzinger (0-based) layout. 6.004 runs information → combinational and sequential logic → pipelining → ISA and procedures → memory hierarchy → processes and synchronization, so operating-system ideas (semaphores) appear inside architecture. 6.006 puts sorting, hashing and graph search in one course. Peterson and Davie's _Computer Networks_ was not fetched because its robots.txt disallows AI agents (Anthropic's among them); no open operating-systems or networks problem set was reached, so those courses have one record each.

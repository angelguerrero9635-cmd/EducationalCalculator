## Order and coverage, in short

- **Calculus I.** Every book teaches limits → derivatives → applications → integrals, but the order
  inside differs: Active Calculus introduces the derivative (1.3) before the limit is formal (1.7);
  CLP-1 puts Taylor polynomials and exponential growth inside "Applications of derivatives"; APEX
  and Stewart give curve sketching and the mean value theorem their own sections (taxonomy gap:
  MVT, Newton's method, curve sketching sit under C1#2 or nowhere). L'Hôpital is a derivative
  application (Active 3.2, CLP 3.7) except in APEX and OpenStax Vol 2, where it follows the
  integration techniques or limits at infinity.
- **Calculus II.** Integration techniques come first everywhere; numerical integration (trapezoid,
  midpoint, Simpson) is a section in every book (Active 5.6, APEX 5.5, CLP-2 1.11, OpenStax V2 3.6)
  and has no taxonomy topic. Centres of mass and hydrostatic force are sections of every book (gap).
  Active Calculus 2nd ed. has no convergence-tests chapter (geometric sums only), so C2#3 relies on
  APEX 9, CLP-2 3 and OpenStax V2 5.
- **Calculus III.** Vector-valued functions, arc length and curvature are a full chapter in every
  book (Active 10, APEX 12, CLP-4 1, Stewart 13); the plan files them under C3#0. Cylindrical and
  spherical coordinates and change of variables are sections everywhere (gap).
- **Differential Equations.** Lebl, Trench and Boyce all add Fourier series, PDEs and boundary-value
  problems (no taxonomy topic), and exact equations, existence and uniqueness, and nonlinear
  systems (beyond DE#3). 18.03SC has no series-solutions unit; it teaches complex numbers and
  sinusoids before second-order equations.
- **Linear Algebra.** Strang (and 18.06) start from Ax = b and the four subspaces; Lay puts linear
  transformations in chapter 1; Austin teaches determinants after bases; Beezer leaves
  transformations to the end. Symmetric matrices, quadratic forms and the SVD are a chapter in
  Austin, Lay and Strang (gap).
